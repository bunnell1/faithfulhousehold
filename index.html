A stewardship planning aid

Give first. Then keep the house in order.

One month at a time. The order does not change: Give, Owe, Keep, Live. Change the numbers and the next step changes with them.

  1. 01

    Give

    First fruits, before the rest of the month is spent.

    Giving comes first because Scripture treats the firstfruits as the Lord’s portion (Proverbs 3:9). The month begins with what is set apart, not with what is left after other things. A named amount — even a small one — keeps the heart oriented toward the Giver rather than the remainder. This is not a tax the tool collects; it is a free act of worship that shapes the rest of the order.

  2. 02

    Owe

    What must leave. A payment named, not a worry left vague.

    What is owed is faced next (Proverbs 22:7). A payment named is a debt confronted; a payment left vague is a worry that still owns the month. Naming the balance and the amount that must go out this month makes room for it after giving. Extra that fits can go toward the smallest balance. When a balance reaches zero, its payment is free for the next.

  3. 03

    Keep

    A reserve, taken only from what is still there.

    A reserve is kept from what remains after giving and what is owed (Proverbs 21:20). It is margin for the household, not a pile borrowed from the future. The aim is often one month of living costs. It is taken only from what is still there after the first two steps; it does not come before them.

  4. 04

    Live

    The remainder. It is not the starting point.

    Living is the remainder after giving, what is owed, and a reserve (1 Timothy 6:6). It is the boundary of the month, not the starting point for new wants. Contentment is found inside that boundary. The page shows the division and names one next step; it does not invent needs for what is left.

    A planning aid, not a financial product.

    Grace & Truth Steward shows one month's finances in a biblical order: giving first, then what is owed, then a reserve, then living on what remains. It is meant to be used with Scripture open, alongside prayer, the local church, and counsel you already trust.

    What this site is for

    • Put this month’s take-home into an order you can see.
    • Receive one next step, with a passage, instead of a pile of advice.
    • Mark three intentions yourself. The site does not decide them.
    • Enter a filename and save. Open that file later and it loads back onto the plan.
    • Print the month and keep the paper.

    What this site is not

    • Not financial, tax, or investment advice.
    • Not a bank connection, a full budget, or a place that holds your accounts.
    • Not a substitute for pastoral counsel or a qualified professional.
    • Entered numbers stay on your device. They are not uploaded to the website. Do not put account numbers, full names, or anything that identifies a person in the saved file.

    Why this order

    The order is not a budget system. It is the shape Scripture already gives to a household’s money.

    • Give first. “Honour the LORD with thy substance, and with the firstfruits of all thine increase” (Proverbs 3:9). The month begins with what is set apart, not with what is left.
    • Then what is owed. “The borrower is servant to the lender” (Proverbs 22:7). A payment named is a debt faced. A payment left vague is a worry that still owns the month.
    • Then a reserve. “There is treasure to be desired and oil in the dwelling of the wise; but a foolish man spendeth it up” (Proverbs 21:20). Margin is kept from what remains after giving and paying, not borrowed from the future.
    • Then live on what is left. “But godliness with contentment is great gain” (1 Timothy 6:6). The remainder is the boundary of the month, not the starting point for new wants.

    Knowing the numbers is part of the same care: “Be thou diligent to know the state of thy flocks, and look well to thy herds” (Proverbs 27:23). The page shows one month in that order and names one next step. It does not replace prayer, the local church, or counsel you already trust.

    Where the numbers live

    The plan in this browser is kept in local storage on this device. We do not receive it. Clearing site data, or opening another browser, removes that local copy. A saved file is the copy you can move. Opening it loads the amounts back onto this form.
